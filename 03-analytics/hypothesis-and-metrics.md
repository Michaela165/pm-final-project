# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A heavy BNPL user who already runs two or more pay later apps and checks them before payday.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** See in one place what they owe in total, and when, before payday.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** They can’t see what they owe on the card and on regular pay later orders together (N-07). Balances sit on two screens (T-103), and two reminders for different amounts arrive in the same week (T-104).
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Open each pay later app (Riverty card, Riverty pay later orders, Klarna, PayPal) and read each balance and due date. Copy the figures into a note, a spreadsheet or their head, or take screenshots. Open the bank app to compare the total against the expected balance on payday. Re-check after each reminder, because the amounts differ (T-104) and it isn’t clear which total is real (T-103). Fall back to the debit card for purchases when unsure, as in N-06.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** We must stop Lena from defaulting to rivals and her debit card by giving her one reliable, in-store-capable Riverty card that shows what she owes in a single place, so Riverty owns an everyday relationship instead of relying on whatever checkout happens to show.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For heavy BNPL users like Lena, we will give one Riverty card that works in-store and shows everything she owes in one place, because rivals’ cards are already winning her everyday spending and every month Riverty waits (plus the months of regulatory approval) leaves its growth dependent on whatever checkout happens to show.

That is Lena. But the segment data has no decline, drop-off or churn cut, so “carrying the pain” is inference, not proven.

They have the most pay later orders, so the split balance (T-103, N-07) and the mismatched due dates (T-104) likely hit them hardest. N-02 also says a third app is one more thing to check. Heavy is the only segment where instalments are material (25.4%), which fits Lena’s “money leaves after I keep it” need.

## Step 2 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** N-06 said the card was declined at a bakery, the app did not say why, and they went back to their debit card the same week.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** The Month 4 in-store decline rate was 8.7% (206 attempts), and the test log shows 48 in-store declines (T-101), against a 2.2% to 3.5% overall decline rate.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Lena, 32, a heavy BNPL user who wants money to leave her account only after she keeps the item, hits an unexplained decline at the till and falls back to her debit card. The data shows the decline friction, but nothing splits declines by segment, so this link to Lena is inference.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Lena cannot tell why her card was declined or what to do next, so she abandons it for her bank card at the moment the card is supposed to win.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Showing a decline reason and retry hint should keep people using the card after a failed payment, lifting month-1 and month-2 retention (now 64-69% and 51-57%) and in turn interchange revenue, though the size of the effect is unproven.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The in-store decline rate, 8.7% in Month 4 and 6.4% at its best in Month 3. A second option is the share of declined users who purchase again within 7 days, but that data is not in the pack today.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Month-1 retention must stay at or above 64%, the lowest cohort value observed, so the fix does not cost the existing base.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** I propose two full monthly cohorts after the fix, with a minimum to proceed of an in-store decline rate at or below 6.4% and month-1 retention at or above 64%. Scale if both hold, pivot if the decline rate drops but retention doesn’t move, and kill if neither moves. Please check how long approval takes (N-11), since a message change may or may not need a proposition review.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on N-06’s unexplained bakery decline, after which they went back to their debit card that week, and the Month 4 in-store decline rate of 8.7% (T-101: 48 in-store declines), I believe that showing a decline reason and retry hint for Lena, a heavy BNPL user who pays with whichever option she trusts, will keep her using the card after a failed payment, as measured by a [X]% relative reduction in the in-store decline rate from 8.7% to [6.4% or lower]. I will protect month-1 retention at or above 64% and will make a go/no-go decision after two full monthly cohorts post-fix.
